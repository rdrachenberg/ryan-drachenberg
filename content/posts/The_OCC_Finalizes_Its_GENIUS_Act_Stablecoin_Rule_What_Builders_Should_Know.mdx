The Office of the Comptroller of the Currency has finalized its rule implementing the GENIUS Act, the US federal framework for payment stablecoins. According to the OCC's October 21 news release, the rule sets requirements for permitted payment stablecoin issuers and foreign payment stablecoin issuers under its jurisdiction, along with certain custody activities at OCC-supervised institutions. The Block reports the OCC is the first regulator to finalize its part of the law.

If you build payments, wallets, or anything that touches a dollar stablecoin, the details of who can issue, how reserves work, and who may pay rewards shape your product roadmap.

## What the rule covers

The OCC's bulletin 2026-50 says most of the rule lives in a new 12 CFR Part 15. Topics include:

- Permitted activities and reserve assets
- Redemption and risk management
- Audits, reports, and supervision
- Custody
- Applications and registrations
- Examination and supervision of foreign issuers
- Revocation or rescission of approval in certain cases
- Capital and an operational backstop

The rule applies to national banks and federal savings associations and their subsidiaries, federal branches, foreign payment stablecoin issuers, nonbank applicants or approved federal qualified issuers, and state qualified issuers under OCC authority. It also amends several existing OCC regulations, including capital standards and fee assessments.

## What it leaves out

Anti-money laundering is not part of this rule. The OCC says Bank Secrecy Act, AML, and OFAC sanctions requirements will come in a separate rulemaking in coordination with the Treasury Department. For builders, that means compliance obligations for issuers are still incomplete.

## Timeline

The rule takes effect January 18, 2027, the same date as the GENIUS Act itself, according to the OCC. Decrypt notes that regulators missed the law's July deadline for final rules, and that the OCC's original proposal came in February with a 60-day comment period. The OCC says it will announce separately when it begins accepting applications and waiver requests from prospective issuers. It also received what it called hundreds of comments from crypto firms, community bankers, trade groups, and academics.

## The rewards question

The most contested issue is whether stablecoin holders can earn yield. The Block reports that the proposed rule barred issuers from directly paying interest and presumed that some arrangements with third parties could be unlawful. According to The Block, the final rule drops that third-party presumption, and rewards will be reviewed case by case. An OCC official quoted by The Block said that if the agency sees a clear evasion, it could pursue it.

The Block also notes the GENIUS Act does not bar platforms such as Coinbase from offering rewards, while banks argue such rewards could pull deposits from community institutions. That tension was a major sticking point in the stalled CLARITY Act. We have not verified the rule text itself on this point, so treat the characterization as The Block's reporting rather than the OCC's.

## Takeaways for developers

- **Plan around January 18, 2027.** That is the effective date for both the law and the OCC rule.
- **Expect reserve and redemption standards to be examined.** The OCC describes the rule as setting strong standards for reserve backing, redemption, and risk management. Read the full rule text before designing issuer-facing integrations.
- **Do not assume rewards are settled.** Case-by-case review means product design around yield-like features carries regulatory uncertainty.
- **Watch the AML rulemaking.** Transaction monitoring and sanctions screening obligations are still to come.
- **Other agencies are still working.** The Block reports other regulators have yet to finish their own parts of the law.
