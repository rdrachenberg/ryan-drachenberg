Perpetual futures grew up on offshore crypto venues. This week the US regulator moved to bring a version of them onshore for individual stocks. On November 3 CFTC Chairman Michael Selig released a statement saying perpetual futures on individual equity securities can trade for the first time.

## What the statement says

According to the CFTC's November 3 release, the contracts, which the statement calls Equity Perps, can trade on designated contract markets that are also registered with the SEC as national securities exchanges for security futures. The Commission deemed them approved under Commission Regulation 40.3 as security futures products under the Commodity Futures Modernization Act of 2000. As a result they fall under the Commodity Exchange Act, the federal securities laws, and the rules of both agencies.

The statement frames this as an extension of earlier work: it says the CFTC approved the first bitcoin perpetual futures contract on a CFTC-regulated exchange on May 29, 2026, and that this step carries perps from digital commodities into traditional securities. It also cites the CFTC-SEC memorandum of understanding from March 2026 and argues US participants should be able to use these tools in a regulated framework rather than on offshore, often unregulated venues.

The Block reported the same day that Selig described the framework as recognizing the complementary roles of the CFTC and SEC, and that Selig said both agencies would oversee the contracts.

## What it does not say

The statement as published does not name exchanges, give launch dates, or detail margin rules, position limits or eligible participants. The Block's coverage likewise reports no launch timetable. Product terms will come from exchange rule filings, so treat this as a legal pathway rather than a live market.

## Why builders should care

- **Structure matters.** Perpetuals have no expiry, and The Block notes they let traders take price exposure without owning the asset. Classifying these as security futures puts them inside a long-established dual-regulator regime.
- **Onchain venues are a reference point.** The Block notes that perps have grown in popularity over the past year and that venues such as Hyperliquid trade around the clock. Whether US-listed versions adopt similar mechanics, such as funding rates and continuous trading, will depend on exchange filings.
- **Tokenization overlap.** Teams building tokenized equity products or derivatives infrastructure should watch how the two agencies treat adjacent products, since the same joint framework is being applied across asset types.
- **Data and risk tooling.** If these products launch, demand will grow for funding, basis and open-interest data feeds that handle 24-hour markets.

## What to watch

Look for exchange rule filings naming the first venue, any SEC or CFTC follow-up guidance on margin and surveillance, and whether other perpetual products, such as the oil contracts The Block says the CFTC sought comment on in June, follow the same path.

This is a regulatory development, not a view on any product's merits or prospects.
