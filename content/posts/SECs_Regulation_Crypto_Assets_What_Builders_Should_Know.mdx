If you have ever shipped a token alongside a protocol, you know the awkward question: is this an offering of securities? The SEC has now put forward a rule that tries to give a structured answer. With the comment window closing on October 20, this is the moment when builders can still influence the final text.

## What was proposed

According to the SEC's August 18, 2026 press release, the Commission proposed "Regulation Crypto Assets", a framework for offerings of investment contracts tied to crypto assets. SEC Chairman Paul S. Atkins said in that release that the regulation "seeks to provide crypto asset entrepreneurs and market participants with clear pathways to raise capital under the federal securities laws."

The SEC's rulemaking page lists the release as 33-11434 and 34-106150, under file number S7-2026-27. It says the proposal was published in the Federal Register on August 21, 2026, and that comments are due by October 20, 2026.

## The two exemptions

The proposal creates two exemptions from registration:

- A startup exemption allowing offerings of up to 5 million dollars during a four-year period.
- A fundraising exemption allowing up to 75 million dollars during each 12-month period.

Both would require principles-based narrative disclosures to investors. The larger exemption would also require financial statements and ongoing reporting. Antifraud protections remain in place under the proposal, according to the SEC's rulemaking page.

## The safe harbor

The part developers may care about most is the conditional safe harbor. Per the press release, once an issuer completes or permanently ceases the essential managerial efforts it represented it would undertake, the crypto asset would no longer be subject to classification as an investment contract.

In plain terms, the proposal contemplates a path where a token can stop being tied to the original offering once the team has finished delivering what it promised. The details and conditions sit in the full release, which we encourage you to read directly rather than rely on a summary.

## State preemption

The SEC says the rules would preempt state securities law registration and qualification requirements for offers and sales under Regulation Crypto Assets, as well as for certain secondary market transactions. For teams that today face a patchwork of state rules, that is a notable design choice.

## What a builder should take from this

This is a proposal, not a final rule. Nothing here changes your obligations today, and the final version could differ. Still, there are practical steps worth taking:

- Read the full release and map your own token design against the "essential managerial efforts" language. Roadmaps, treasury control, and admin keys may all become relevant to that analysis.
- Think about which exemption tier would fit your fundraising plans, and what disclosure and reporting you would need to sustain.
- Talk to counsel before relying on any part of the proposal.
- If you have concrete operational concerns, submit a comment through the SEC's portal for S7-2026-27 before October 20.

## The bigger picture

For years, the dominant complaint from crypto teams was the absence of a workable on-ramp for compliant token offerings. This proposal is an explicit attempt to build one. Whether the thresholds, disclosure burden, and safe harbor conditions are practical is exactly what the comment period is meant to test.
