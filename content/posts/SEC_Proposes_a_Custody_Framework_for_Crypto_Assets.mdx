On October 1, 2026, the SEC proposed new rules and amendments aimed at how registered investment advisers and regulated funds hold crypto assets. It is a proposal, not a final rule, but custody is one of the biggest practical blockers for institutional crypto products, so the details matter to anyone building wallets, custody tooling or fund infrastructure.

## What the SEC announced

According to the SEC's press release (2026-100), the proposal would create a tailored framework for custody of crypto assets by registered investment advisers and by regulated funds, which the release defines as registered investment companies and business development companies. The stated goals are to modernize custody rules, expand investor choice and remove barriers to advisers giving crypto-related advice.

The release says the proposal amends rules under the Advisers Act and the Investment Company Act of 1940. Its main elements, as described by the SEC:

- Rule updates that reflect current industry practices.
- Revised requirements around financial statement audits for registered investment advisers.
- Revised requirements around broker-dealer custodial services for regulated funds.
- Crypto assets could be held in self-custody in certain circumstances.
- State trust companies could serve as custodians for client and regulated fund crypto assets.

SEC Chairman Paul S. Atkins said the proposal "would provide a clear regulatory framework for the custody of crypto assets," and described it as replacing "the grey of uncertainty created by custody rules crafted for a bygone era." The release links to a fact sheet and the full proposed rule, titled "Investment Adviser and Regulated Fund Custody Rules; Crypto Custody."

## Process and timing

The SEC says the public comment period will stay open for 60 days after the proposing release is published in the Federal Register. The press release does not give a closing date, and it does not report a Commission vote count, so we will not guess at either. Nothing here changes obligations today.

## Why builders should care

Custody rules were written for stock certificates and bank accounts, and they have long pushed advisers toward a small set of qualified custodians. Two parts of this proposal stand out for developers:

- **Self-custody in limited cases.** If the final rule allows it, the technical details of how control over keys is demonstrated, audited and recovered become a product requirement. Key management, multi-party computation and signing policies could matter in compliance conversations.
- **State trust companies as custodians.** This could widen the pool of custodians that fund managers integrate with, which in turn means more APIs, reporting formats and attestations to support.

The audit-related changes also point to a need for clean, verifiable records of holdings and transfers, something blockchains are good at but that still needs reconciliation tooling.

## What to do now

We suggest reading the proposed rule itself rather than summaries, since the release is a high-level description. If your product touches custody, comment filing is open to anyone once the Federal Register clock starts. Track the Federal Register publication date, because the 60-day window runs from it.

Keep in mind this is early. Proposals change, sometimes substantially, between draft and final form.
