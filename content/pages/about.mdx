I build software for the web, and I've been at it long enough to know that "it works on my machine" is not a deployment strategy. My work spans full-stack development, blockchain, and AI: Next.js front ends, APIs and databases, smart contracts, and, more and more, applications that put large language models to real work. What ties it together is a simple goal: build things that solve an actual problem and are a pleasure to use.

My recent projects show the range. Sol Kat Boutique is a resale storefront with inventory tracking, eBay price comparisons, and Stripe checkout. BG Remover strips image backgrounds entirely in the browser, so photos never leave the user's device. Solana Mint Forge lets anyone mint their own token on Solana, and Rip Cord loads any EVM smart contract and makes every one of its functions callable. Each one went from idea to production, which is where the interesting problems tend to show up.

Lately I've been going deep on AI engineering and have completed Anthropic's coursework on building with Claude. Along the way I built a mock exam and study guide for the Claude Certified Architect – Foundations exam, because the best way to learn something is to build a tool that quizzes you on it.

I'm committed to continuous learning, mostly because this industry doesn't leave much choice: the framework you mastered last year shipped a new major version this morning. I pair that curiosity with discipline: clean, maintainable code, security treated as a requirement rather than an afterthought, and clear communication with the people I'm building for. If you have a project in mind, or just want to talk shop about web3 or AI, reach out through the links on this page.
